Project overview

The product

Check8 is a web-based QR clearance portal for Gordon College Computer Science students. It replaces paper clearance slips with online tracking: students check their pending items on their phone and clear each station with one scan.

Target users

Gordon College CCS students, lab staff, library personnel, student council officers, and faculty signatories.

Project duration

April 2026 to November 2026.

The problem

Every semester, students wait in long lines for paper signatures, and losing the slip forces them to start over.

The goal

Show cleared and pending stations on one phone screen, with a scannable QR pass for fast sign-offs.

Understanding the user

User research summary

We interviewed 6 BSCS students and 2 office staff. Students could not see which items were still missing, and staff disliked looking up records by hand in binders.

Pain points

1. Waiting in line blindStudents wait 30 minutes before learning an item is missing, so the office checklist sits front and center.
2. Lost paper slipsPaper forms tear or go missing during finals week. An online QR pass can't be lost.
3. Vague "Pending" labelsUnexplained holds confuse students, so a short instructor note sits beside each pending item.
4. Slow record lookupsChecking paper lists wastes staff time, so live QR scanning comes with a manual Student ID lookup.

Persona and problem statement

Marco Reyes, 21 (an illustrative persona based on our target users)

Who: a graduating 4th-year BSCS student from Olongapo City and the eldest sibling supporting his family.

Goals: see every clearance station on one phone screen, show a scannable QR pass for quick sign-offs, and know why a station is pending before lining up.

Frustrations: crumpled or lost paper slips, waiting in line before finding out something is missing, and not knowing which office is holding up his clearance.

Quote: "Chasing paper signatures across campus during finals week wastes hours when nobody tells you what's missing."

Usability study: findings

We ran pilot tests with 4 classmates over two rounds.

Round 1
  1. Status colors alone were unclear, so they needed Cleared and Pending text labels.
  2. The camera scanner needed a manual Student ID lookup as a backup.
  3. Instructor notes needed to sit right next to the pending rows.
Round 2
  1. The QR code needed a bright white card to scan reliably in low light.
  2. Students and instructors needed separate views, each with only their own tools.
  3. Approvals needed timestamps for a clear record.

Accessibility considerations

1. Words alongside colorStatus pills pair Cleared and Pending text with distinct borders, so no one relies on color.
2. Thumb-friendly targetsButtons and tabs are sized for easy one-handed taps on a phone.
3. Clean stacking on small screensTables and QR cards stack vertically with no sideways scrolling.

Takeaways

Impact

Check8 gives the department a dedicated clearance system that replaces fragile paper slips with trackable QR verification.

What I learned

  • Writing the requirements first made building faster. Once each office's steps were mapped into clear states, the code mostly followed.
  • Leading implementation in a shared repo taught me to split work into small pieces and merge often so teammates don't block each other.
  • Every automated step needs a manual backup. The Student ID lookup saved scans whenever a camera or screen failed.
  • Good documentation helps the whole team, not just graders. It kept everyone using the same names for the same features.
